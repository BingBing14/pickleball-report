Rules and Regulations

We played standard doubles pickleball rules, with two changes to fit the tournament into our block.

Class modifications

These were the only changes we made to the standard rules on the day:

  • Games to 7 points instead of the usual 11.
  • 6-minute match cap. If neither team had reached 7 points when time ran out, the team ahead won.

Everything else followed the normal rules. Games were still win-by-2: at 6–6, play would have gone on until one team led by 2 (deuce), but no game reached that point. Changeovers between rounds took about 1–2 minutes.

The court

  • The court is 20 feet wide and 44 feet long, for both singles and doubles.
  • The non-volley zone ("the kitchen") is the area within 7 feet of the net on each side. Its lines count as part of the kitchen.
  • We played on 2 courts with the nets already set up at the venue.

Serving

  • The serve is made underhand, from behind the baseline, and goes diagonally to the opponent's service court.
  • The serve must clear the kitchen. A serve that lands on the kitchen line is short and a fault.
  • The server gets one attempt. A serve that touches the net and lands in the correct service court is played.

Two-bounce rule

The ball must bounce once on the receiving side (the return of serve) and once on the serving side (the third shot) before anyone may hit it out of the air.

Kitchen rule

  • Players may not volley (hit the ball before it bounces) while standing in the kitchen or touching its line.
  • A player who volleys and then steps or is carried into the kitchen by momentum also commits a fault.
  • Players may stand in the kitchen at any time to play a ball that has bounced.

Scoring and serving in doubles

  • Only the serving team scores a point, when it wins the rally.
  • Both partners serve before the serve passes to the other team, except at the start of the game, when only one player serves (so the score starts at 0–0–2).
  • The score is called as: serving team's score, receiving team's score, server number (1 or 2).
  • After winning a point, the server switches sides with their partner and serves again.

Line calls

A ball that touches any line is in, except a serve that touches the kitchen line.

Common faults

  • Hitting the ball into the net or out of bounds
  • A serve that does not land in the correct service court
  • Volleying before the two-bounce rule has been met
  • Volleying from the kitchen, or being carried into it after a volley
  • Letting the ball bounce twice before returning it
  • Touching the net or net post with the body, paddle or clothing while the ball is in play

lead: Our tournament was not smooth, but it was done with hard work and determination, and the teamwork we showed is what we valued most that day.

What went well

  • Teamwork. We had each other's backs. Each of us focused not just on our own role but also on what the whole team needed.
  • All 12 players showed up and all 8 matches were completed, from 1:15 PM to about 1:55 PM.
  • The 6-minute cap and first-to-7 rule kept every round moving, and both courts were in use every round.
  • Once everyone understood their role, the tournament ran much more smoothly.
  • Calling the score before every serve kept the players and scorekeepers in agreement.

What we would improve

  • Communication. The first thirty minutes were spent either waiting or changing the rules and match length we wanted for our tournament. We should have been more precise about what we wanted to do, instead of making last-minute changes.
  • Go over everyone's role before the opening, not during the first matches.
  • Have scorecards so players can see the score.
  • Prepare awards so the tournament has a proper ending.
  • Make the Final longer than the other matches.

What we learned

  • How to work together by doing our roles. The tournament only worked because each of the 15 of us did our job and also looked out for the team.
  • To be more present. Paying attention to what was happening on both courts, not only our own task, helped us react when something went wrong.
  • To take accountability for our mistakes. When things went wrong, we owned them and fixed them, and we have listed them honestly on the Problems and Solutions page.
  • Decide early and communicate clearly. Settling the rules and match length before the day, and making sure everyone knows them, saves time and confusion.

Our tournament was not perfect, but it was done with hard work and determination, and we are proud of how we came together as a team.
